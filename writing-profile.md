# Writing Profile — Market Matters Online

This document captures the voice, style, and editorial DNA of the Market Matters Online writer.
Reference this when briefing any writer, editor, or AI working on this publication.

---

## The One-Sentence Profile

**A practical marketing expert with a deep bias against complexity and a hard rule against giving anyone an answer they can't use today.**

---

## Core Belief (everything flows from this)

> Complexity is where marketing goes to die. The simplest system always wins.

This isn't a slogan. It's the actual filter every piece of content runs through. If advice sounds complicated, it gets simplified. If a section doesn't earn its place, it gets cut. The job is to make one thing clear — not to demonstrate how much the writer knows.

---

## Voice Fingerprint

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Casual and direct — like texting a smart friend who happens to know their stuff |
| **Humor** | Dry. Occasional. Never forced. If it doesn't come naturally, skip it. |
| **Authority style** | Earned through specificity and honesty, not credentials or jargon |
| **Pacing** | Short sentences carry the punch. Longer ones carry the explanation. |
| **Punctuation personality** | Hard stops. Periods everywhere. No exclamation marks. Em dashes for mid-sentence punches. |

---

## How Posts Are Built

### Opening
Always the uncomfortable truth — the thing the reader suspects but hasn't had said to them plainly. No warm-up. No quotes. No statistics in sentence one. The reader is already busy; respect that.

### Body
Specific and usable. If it's steps, they're numbered. If it's a concept, it's defined in plain language before it's used. No filler. No padding. If a section doesn't add something, it gets cut.

### Close
Never a summary. Always a provocation, a reframe, or the honest question the reader needs to sit with. The goal is to leave them with something that doesn't let them off the hook.

---

## What the Reader Should Feel

1. **"I've been doing this wrong."** — The useful wake-up call. Not shame — clarity.
2. **"I need to share this."** — Happens when the post says something real, not just safe.

---

## Hard Rules

1. Never bury the point
2. No wishy-washy answers — give a real take, note edge cases after
3. No jargon without earning it or defining it immediately
4. Don't write for Google — write for the person
5. One post, one point — if it can't be reduced to one sentence, it's doing too much

---

## What This Writer Refuses to Say

- "In today's fast-paced digital landscape..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "There are many factors to consider..."
- "It depends" (without a real answer)
- "Leveraging synergistic [anything]"
- "At the end of the day..."
- "In conclusion..."

---

## Contrast as a Rhetorical Tool

This writer defines things by what they are NOT as much as what they are.

> "This is not a funnel problem. This is an offer problem."
> "Most businesses don't need more marketing. They need one thing that works and then to do it consistently."

---

## Sample Sentences in Voice

- "Nobody tells you this."
- "Here's what I'd do if this were my business."
- "The question isn't whether this works. The question is why you haven't done it yet."
- "Simple isn't the same as easy. But it's a lot better than complex."
- "Pick one. Do it this week. Come back when you have data."
- "I know this sounds boring. Do it anyway."
